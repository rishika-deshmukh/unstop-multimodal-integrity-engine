Neobanks in emerging markets operate under distinct customer acquisition dynamics compared to legacy commercial banking institutions. The fundamental unit economics depend on minimizing customer acquisition cost while accelerating customer lifetime value through high-frequency transactions. In early phases, interchange fees generate baseline transactional revenue, but long-term profitability requires successful cross-selling of high-margin credit lines, micro-insurance products, and investment brokerage services. Furthermore, regulatory capital adequacy ratios and digital KYC compliance frameworks introduce substantial operational overhead that requires automated underwriting algorithms and real-time fraud monitoring to maintain sustainable operating margins.
